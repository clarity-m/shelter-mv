The final frame is done: `style-frames/14-pixel-swarm/frame.png`. It shows Alpha Centauri B inside three nested shells of dark collector panels, drawn in the 09 lab's pixel craft (480×270, 32 colours scaled up 4×) with passes 1–7, `thumb.png` and `NOTES.md` alongside. Each shell leaves an opening where the camera came out, so you look down into the warm interior to the white star, with the pastel sunset bubble in a porthole beside it. Eleven lanes leave hot notches in the rim and fan out as uneven tapered shafts that form the spark; A sits upper left, and the Sun is a faint point lower left at the end of Cassiopeia's W.

It reads as an icon at 320×180, and 91% of the frame is below 10% luminance. It renders in about 0.86 s of script per frame (about 3 s including Chrome startup), and re-renders are byte-identical. What falls short: the nesting shows only through the opening, where the inner shells blur into a warm mottle, and the ray tails come out rust-maroon because the lab's warm colours turn brown at low brightness. I tried cutting the lanes across the lantern's face in pass 2: the spark read more strongly, but the sphere looked like shattered glass.

My honest view is that pixel art makes a strong icon here, but I'd hesitate to use it for the whole outside world. It duplicates the inside's pixel rung, so the early intercuts would differ only in palette. And at S32 the camera leaves a world of light for a 480×270 reality, which reads as a downgrade of the real world.
