
## YOUR SHOT: the swarm, end of the final pull-back (S32 to S33, about 3:31)

The last image before the closing starburst. The camera has just pulled all the
way back, out of the simulated world, out of the swarm, to see the whole thing: a
Dyson swarm around Alpha Centauri B, and the world it holds. Here, reading as an
icon is the point.

Build the swarm as a lantern. It is nested shells of dark paper panels, the swarm's
collectors, around the star. Each shell is perforated with gaps between its panels.
Seen from the front, the shells are concentric layers, each cut with a different
pattern of gaps. The star's light escapes through the gaps: a warm glow through
every small hole. Where gaps line up through all the shells, light escapes fully
and fans out into the surrounding haze as long soft shafts. There are ELEVEN of
these rays, uneven in length and width, and they form the Claude spark, an
irregular radial burst of about 11 tapered rays. The spark must emerge from the
physics of light through gaps, not be pasted on.

Inside, glimpsed through one larger gap near the star, is a small warm bubble: the
simulated world the swarm hosts. It is a tiny luminous sphere with a hint of a hill
and a sunset in it, the only pastel in the frame.

Around it:
- Alpha Centauri A is a bright pinhole with a soft glow, upper left and well away
  from the swarm.
- The Sun is a faint, tiny pinhole lower left, barely there: home, far away.
- Space is near-black paper with pinhole stars on two or three layers at different
  depths (ready for parallax), and a faint Milky Way band in translucent tissue.

Avoid what failed before (read `../04b-swarm-contrast/NOTES.md`): crossing orbital
bands make the swarm read as an atom or armillary sphere. This is a shell, a
lantern, not rings. No HUD, no text.

The warm light should be the brightest thing in the film, but concentrated: a star
held in paper, with most of the frame dark.
