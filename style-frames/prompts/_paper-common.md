
## YOUR STYLE: backlit cut paper (the lantern world)

The candidate: the real world is built of layered cut paper, like a backlit
shadow box (layered cut-paper lightbox art). Light shines through holes, gaps and
thin paper. The idea behind it: the inside worlds are made of light (Claude's world
model), and the outside world is made of matter. Claude's contribution to the real
world is light passing through that matter. The paper lantern is the film's title
image. A lantern shelters a flame, and the flame lights the lantern. The harness on
the lab bench is a small lantern around Clawd. The city's windows are lanterns. The
Dyson swarm at the end is a lantern around a star. Precedents to think with:
layered paper-cut lightboxes, silhouette animation (Lotte Reiniger), paper
lanterns, and punched-tin lanterns that throw star patterns on walls.

Three agents render three different shots in this style at the same time, so keep
exactly to this spec. The three frames must look like one film.

**Construction.** Each scene is 5-9 flat paper layers at different depths, parallel
to the image plane, like a shadow box. Each layer is a cut shape. Opaque card
blocks light. Translucent vellum or tissue transmits it. Holes pass it at full
strength. Build it so a camera could later move in 2.5D, with parallax between the
layers.

**Papers.** Opaque cards, near to far:
- #10131F ink blue
- #171C30 deep indigo
- #212843 slate indigo
- #2D3656 dusk slate
- #3B4668 far slate

The backmost layer is either a night-sky tissue, a cool gradient from #1B2138 at
the top to #34406A at the horizon, or, in space, near-black paper from #07080E to
#0E1120. The world is low-saturation cool. Nothing is pure black.

**Light.** There are two kinds, and only two.
- Claude's light is warm orange. Its ramp:
  - #8A3A24 (deep, transmitted through thick paper)
  - #C4603F
  - #D97757 (base)
  - #F0A070
  - #FFD9B8 (hot)
  - #FFF3E6 (core)
- The humans' own light is cool, small and dim: moonlight, screens, indicator
  LEDs. Keep it around #9FB3D9, with highlights at most #D8E2F5.

**Paper physics.** These are the signature of the style; get them right.
- Transmitted light reveals the paper's fibre. Where light comes through vellum or
  tissue, show mottled fibre density: fine anisotropic fibres plus soft cloudy
  variation. Lit paper must look like paper, not like a gradient.
- Raking light reveals the surface. Opaque card facing a light shows faint fibre
  texture. In darkness it is nearly flat.
- Cut edges are slightly hand-cut, with sub-pixel wobble of about 0.5-1 px and the
  occasional tiny notch. Give them a 1-2 px rim: bright on edges facing a light
  source, dark on the others.
- Each layer casts a soft shadow on the layers behind it. The shadow is offset away
  from the dominant light, and its blur grows with the depth gap.
- Thin paper glows at its edges when backlit, the way fingers glow red when you
  cover a torch.
- Air between the layers carries a faint haze. Light shafts appear only where
  light passes through holes or gaps. They are subtle and volumetric, and never
  laser beams.

**The photograph.** Treat the image as a real shadow box photographed at night:
- gentle bloom only on the hottest holes;
- slightly soft focus on the nearest layer;
- fine grain of about 3%;
- a faint vignette.

**Humans (lab frame only).** They are opaque silhouettes, faceless by construction.
Readability comes from clean posture and negative-space cuts, such as the gap
between arm and body and the shape of the hair. They are rim-lit by the orange
where it reaches them.

**Colour is capability.** The warm light is the only saturated thing, and how much
of the frame it touches is the measure of Claude's impact in that scene. Your
frame's position in the film sets that amount (see your shot).
