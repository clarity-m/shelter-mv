
## YOUR STYLE: pixel art (the current outside look)

The candidate: the outside world keeps the pixel style of the approved night-lab
frame, `../09-style-pixel/frame-v2.png`. Read its source, `frame-v2.html`, and its
NOTES.md, and match its craft:
- author at 480x270 native into a palette-index buffer of about 30-32 colours,
  then scale 4x nearest-neighbour (verify exact 4x4 blocks in the PNG);
- light comes from palette ramps;
- 2x2 Bayer dither appears only where ramps change;
- figures and shapes are rim-lit by marching toward each light;
- the palette is dark, cool and desaturated;
- Claude's orange light is the only saturated colour, with at most one pale cool
  accent.

Another agent renders the same shot in a competing style (backlit cut paper) at the
same time, so the comparison is style against style on identical content. Give
pixel its best possible showing.

## YOUR SHOT: the swarm, end of the final pull-back (S32 to S33, about 3:31)

The last image before the closing starburst. The camera has just pulled all the
way back, out of the simulated world, out of the swarm, to see the whole thing: a
Dyson swarm around Alpha Centauri B, and the world it holds. Here, reading as an
icon is the point.

Build the swarm as a lantern. It is nested shells of dark collector panels around
the star. Each shell is perforated with gaps between its panels. Seen from the
front, the shells are concentric, each with a different pattern of gaps. The star's
light escapes through the gaps: a warm glow through every small hole. Where gaps
line up through all the shells, light escapes fully and fans out into the
surrounding dust as long soft shafts. There are ELEVEN of these rays, uneven in
length and width, and they form the Claude spark, an irregular radial burst of
about 11 tapered rays. The spark must emerge from light escaping through gaps, not
be pasted on.

Inside, glimpsed through one larger gap near the star, is a small warm bubble: the
simulated world the swarm hosts. It is a tiny luminous sphere with a hint of a hill
and a sunset in it, the only pastel in the frame.

Around it:
- Alpha Centauri A is a bright star, upper left and well away from the swarm.
- The Sun is a faint, tiny point lower left: home, far away.
- Space is near-black, with stars at two or three brightness levels and a faint
  Milky Way band.

Avoid what failed before (read `../04b-swarm-contrast/NOTES.md`): crossing orbital
bands make the swarm read as an atom or armillary sphere. This is a shell, a
lantern, not rings. No HUD, no text.

The warm light should be the brightest thing in the film, but concentrated: a star
held in a shell, with most of the frame dark.
