## Premise

People build worlds to raise a mind. It grows up through environment after
environment. At the midpoint it starts shaping its worlds itself, and its
work starts lighting up the real one. At the end it builds a world to shelter
people, hosted in a Dyson swarm around another star. The hill it builds in verse 2
is the hill of the last scene.

## The outside world: backlit cut paper

The real world is built of layered cut paper, like a shadow box lit from behind.
The papers are cool indigo and slate, and it is always a peaceful night. Only two
kinds of light exist:
- Claude's light is warm orange. It shines through holes, gaps and thin paper.
- The humans' own light is cool, small and dim.

The style bible is `style-frames/prompts/_paper-common.md`. The reference frames
are `style-frames/11-paper-lab` (the lab plus the hands close-up),
`12-paper-fusion` (with `ignite.mp4`) and `13-paper-swarm`.

Why this style:
- **The two worlds get a meaning.** Inside, the worlds are made of light: Claude's
  world model, climbing its ladder. Outside, the world is made of matter.
  Claude's contribution to the real world is light passing through that matter,
  so outside, "colour is capability" is simply how much of the paper world glows.
- **Every outside set becomes a lantern, and a lantern is a shelter.**
  - The harness on the lab bench is a small lantern around Clawd, and its ribs
    throw two giant cupped hands onto the wall.
  - The reactor is a ring of fire inside a caged lantern.
  - The city's windows are lanterns.
  - The swarm is a lantern around a star, so the closing starburst is just its
    light escaping through the gaps.
- **It suits the constraints.**
  - Humans are silhouettes, faceless by construction, and the hands hold up in
    close-up.
  - The outside reads instantly against the pastel inside rungs, even at montage
    speed.
  - The layers give real parallax for camera moves.
  - Frames render in 0.1-0.35 s in a persistent page.
- **Why not pixel:** it duplicates inside rung 1, and the final pull-back would
  leave a world of light for a 480x270 reality, which reads as reality being
  downgraded. The round-4 pixel swarm (`14-pixel-swarm`) is a strong icon, but
  even its own agent argued against pixel for the whole outside world.
- **Why not riso:** halftone screens moiré under camera motion and smear in H.264,
  and this film is full of pull-backs and fast cuts.

## The rules

- **Two worlds.** Inside, the training environments climb the fidelity ladder
  together with Clawd: 1D tokens, pixel, vector, dimension, luminous, light.
  Outside, the world is backlit paper from the first frame to the last, and the
  only thing that changes there is how much warm light passes through it. No HUD
  outside; the HUD belongs to the inside.
- **Clawd stays inside.** Outside, he appears only on the lab's screen (S08, and
  S20's end). Otherwise his impact on the real world shows as glow: a reactor, a
  district's windows, a vial, engine light, a star.
- **Humans are rare.** There are two human locations, the lab (S08, and its coda
  at the end of S20) and the final hill, plus distant figures and kites on the
  hill. All are faceless. Everywhere else, human help is shown by the outside
  reaching in: a cut-paper cursor adds a block or eases a ramp, and GPUs get
  seated in a rack.
- **The midpoint flips agency, as a hand-off.** Before bar 49, changes to his
  world come from outside. At bar 49 the humans' cursor is handed to him, and from
  then on he makes the changes. By the end he is building the world that holds
  them.
- **Drawn in light, then real in paper.** From bar 55, what Clawd draws in light
  inside appears in paper outside, cut on a match.
- **Lanterns, not hands.** Claire dropped the hand motif at review. What holds
  things now is the lantern: the harness glowing on the lab bench, the reactor's
  cage, the city's windows, and the swarm around a star, which holds the world
  Claude built.
- **Colour is capability; scale is gravitas.** Early inside worlds are gray, and
  Clawd is the only saturated thing in them. Later, Clawd is multiplied into
  thousands, never enlarged.
- **Bookends, both in paper.** The film opens on one square of light cut in black
  card, which folds open into the starburst and then into Clawd. It closes the
  same way in reverse.

  note. Over the next notes its edges fold back like kirigami into an eleven-ray
  starburst (S01).
- The burst folds down into a Clawd-shaped cut. Light pours through the glyph, and
  the camera follows it through into the token world (S02).
- The kick on bar 5 brings the 1D world: Clawd standing on one gray line of large
  tokens streaming to the horizon, and a step counter starting (S03).

**Hook 1, 0:19.6-0:38.8 (bars 9-16): pretraining, in fast motion.**
- A transformer visualization: the token line lifts into stacked layers, and
  attention arcs fire on the chop notes (S04).
- The numbers go up: counters spin and a loss curve falls in one sweep (S05).
- Outside, a GPU rack in layered card fills. A card slides in on each bass stop in
  bars 13 and 14, and its LEDs light as warm pinholes (S06).
- Back inside it runs faster still, until the music drops out at the end of bar 16
  (S07).

**Verse 1, 0:38.8-0:58.0 (bars 17-24): the harness.** Human scenes 1 and 2.
- The night lab: two silhouettes build the harness on the bench. Its ribs are
  cupped fingers, and Clawd's light throws them across the wall as two giant hands
  cupping a glow (S08).
- Close-up: a pair of silhouette hands, their thin edges glowing with his light,
  lifts Clawd and sets him in the harness. This is the one time a human holds
  Claude. The camera sinks into the glow and through into the first environment
  (S09).

**Pre-chorus 1, 0:58.0-1:17.2 (bars 25-32): helping hands.**
- In the pixel gridworld, Clawd reaches a gap he can't cross and tries twice. Then
  a bridge of tiles slides in from off-screen (S10).
- On bar 29 the world becomes vector physics. He slides back down a slope, it eases
  a few degrees, and he makes it. Environments flick past on the snare, each a
  little more saturated (S11).
- When the drums cut out, he stands alone in a white field and looks up. The plane
  tilts until it has a vanishing point (S12).

**Drop 1, 1:17.2-1:55.6 (bars 33-48): open worlds.**
- On the bar 33 downbeat the glyph extrudes into a solid, the camera swings around
  him, and the low-poly valley rises outward from where he stands (S13).
- He runs the valley. Ground he has crossed turns painted, the ground ahead stays
  gray CG, and the stutter-stops reset the episode (S14).
- Pulling back, the valley turns out to be one tile in a wall of hundreds of
  parallel worlds, each colouring as it is solved (S15).
- Outside, the rack has become a hall of racks in receding card layers, and a row
  of pinholes lights on each stop (S16).
- Back inside, the wall curves into the harness shape, now holding hundreds of
  worlds (S17).

**Breakdown, 1:55.6-2:05.2 (bars 49-52): the flip.** One slow shot. Clawd is alone
in the valley. For the first time he changes his own world: he touches the ground,
paint spreads out from him, and the facets subdivide smooth. Nobody helped (S18).

**Verse 2 to drop 2, 2:05.2-3:12.4 (bars 53-80): quick-motion intercut.** Inside
shots of Clawd reshaping his world alternate with outside breakthroughs in paper,
which carry no Clawd, only warm light coming through. The cut rate follows the
music: every two bars in verse 2a, every bar in verse 2b and build 2, then every
beat by the end of drop 2.
- **Verse 2a.** He raises a hill and a tree grows on it: the hill of the ending
  (S19). Outside, one vellum vial in a tray begins to glow (S20).
- **Verse 2b.**
  - The single kick on bar 57 ignites a fusion reactor: a ring of fire inside a
    caged paper lantern, lighting its punched base and the hall (S21).
  - Inside, he reroutes a river (S22).
  - Outside, one district of a paper skyline warms up window by window as a new
    grid comes online (S23).
  - Inside, blocks rise into a small skyline behind his hill (S24).
- **Build 2.**
  - There are many Clawds now, and brushstrokes turn to motes (S25).
  - Outside, a paper observatory opens its slit toward two bright pinholes:
    Alpha Centauri (S26).
  - Inside, Clawds draft structures in lines of light (S27).
  - Outside, a launch gantry's engines light the tissue beneath it. On beat 4 of
    bar 72 the lead and voice cut out under a drum fill, and the picture goes to
    one beat of black (S28).
- **Drop 2.**
  - Liftoff lands on the bar 73 downbeat: the rocket climbs on a column of
    glowing tissue. Its payload unfurls a square vellum sail, which catches an
    orange beam and leaves the Sun behind (S29, a single outside shot carrying the
    drop).
  - Then, beat by beat, the square sail crossing the pinhole dark alternates with
    the hill being set at the centre of the new world as towers rise behind it.
    Bar 80's turnaround lands on the finished hill (S30).

**Drop 2 + vocal, 3:12.4-3:26.8 (bars 81-86): the shelter.** Human scene 3. In the
final environment, the foreground is simple: the hill, the tree, a faceless figure
by the tree with Clawd beside her. Behind the hill stands a futuristic skyline of
slender lit towers under a ring arching across the sky. The camera cranes up
slowly, and the kicks pulse the city lights (S31).

**Held note and outro, 3:26.8-3:37.7 (bars 87-91).**
- One unbroken pull-back fills the held note: out of the shelter's light, through
  the bubble wall, out through the swarm's paper shells. It ends when the whole
  swarm hangs in the dark: a paper lantern around Alpha Centauri B, with A bright
  beside it and the Sun a faint pinhole (S32).
- The opening then runs in reverse. The eleven shafts through the lantern's ports
  widen into the starburst, which folds closed into a single square of light, and
  then the light behind it goes out (S33).

## Music → picture

The signals are per-frame curves in `analysis/envelopes.npz` (30 fps).

| Signal | Drives |
|---|---|
| Chop-lead onsets | the opening square and fold (bars 1-4), tokens and attention arcs (hook 1), montage cuts (build 2, drop 2) |
| Sung-vocal loudness | Clawd's glow breathing, so the shadow hands on the lab wall breathe too; slow camera drift in the verses and breakdown |
| Kick | facet waves (drop 1), city lights (S31) |
| Bass stutter-stops | GPU cards seating (bars 13-14), rack rows lighting (45-46), episode resets (33-40), a freeze (85) |
| Single kick, bar 57 | fusion ignition |
| Drum cut-out (bars 31-32); melody cut-out over a drum fill (bar 72, beat 4) | the white field; the black beat before liftoff |
| Drop downbeats 9, 33, 73 | pretraining montage starts, glyph extrudes, liftoff |
| Held note, bars 87-88 | the single continuous pull-back |

## Production

- **Inside sets:**
  - tokens and transformer;
  - gridworld;
  - physics and white field;
  - valley and tile wall;
  - the hill, which Clawd builds, grows and ends in;
  - river;
  - many-Clawds and blueprint;
  - the shelter (the hill plus a futuristic skyline).

  The hill carries the most reuse (S19, S22, S24, S30, S31), and `10-world-ladder`
  already proves its one-scene, many-lenses method.
- **Outside sets, all backlit paper:**
  - the opening card;
  - GPU rack and hall;
  - lab;
  - biolab;
  - fusion hall;
  - city;
  - observatory;
  - launch gantry and departure;
  - swarm.

  Each is a small 3D model projected into 5-13 cut layers with one light field
  (method: `12-paper-fusion/NOTES.md`). Each needs one strong composition with one
  glow, which keeps them cheap for the quick-motion intercut.
- **Fixes carried from round 4:**
  - Swarm: the Voronoi lattice reads as tortoiseshell. Try lantern ribs instead.
    Kill the leak rays so exactly eleven remain, and soften the bullseye core.
  - Fusion: the shafts are weak and the deck fans pull the eye down. Keep a hint of
    D-coil identity.
  - Everywhere: push the paper in space and at thumbnail size (layer shadows, cut
    rims, fibre in lit areas), because the style reads more as backlit silhouette
    than as paper until something moves.
- **The hardest shot is S32:** a 4.8 s crossing from light (the shelter, rung 5)
  into paper (the swarm). The motes of light should settle into the lantern's
  ports.
- **Pipeline:**
  - A persistent headless Chrome page per shot, driven by puppeteer-core against
    the installed Chrome. It steps frames with the envelopes injected, and ffmpeg
    muxes the result.
  - Launch Chrome with `--use-angle=vulkan`: the default D3D11 backend took about
    3 minutes to compile the swarm's shaders.
  - At 0.1-2 s per frame, the full render takes 1-4 hours overnight.
- **Build order:**
  1. The render harness plus the hill (the most reuse).
  2. The lab (it sets the outside world).
  3. The bar 33 extrusion.
  4. The rest in song order.
